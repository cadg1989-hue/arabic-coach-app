How this method makes words stick (and come out of your mouth)
  1. Hook it: each new word gets a sound-alike memory hook and, where one exists, the Hindi/Urdu word it shares (waqt, jism…).
  2. Hear it in use: a real Saudi work sentence, and you shadow it, speaking along with the audio.
  3. Pull it from memory: you answer before you see the answer. The effort of recalling is what builds memory.
  4. Climb the ladder: recognise it → hear it → say the word → say a whole sentence with it. Saying it yourself is the hardest step, so the drills move you there.
  5. Space it: words return just before you'd forget them. Rate yourself honestly (Again / Hard / Easy) and the timing adjusts.
  6. Use it for real: each session ends with 3 sentences to say to a real person today. Your Drive and car lessons recycle the same words.

One verb, every person

Same word, different endings

tiji, tijeen, tijoon, yijoon… it's all one verb, "come". The start and end of the word tell you who. Learn this pattern once and it works for almost every verb.

WhoNowPast ana · Ia……t inta · you (m)t……t inti · you (f)t…een…ti huwa · hey…(plain) hiya · shet……at ihna · wen……na intum · you allt…oon…tu hum · theyy…oon…aw

Negative: put ma in front (ma aji = I don't come). "Don't!" orders use la (la tiji = don't come).
